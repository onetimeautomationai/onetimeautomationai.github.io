What is inside Anthropic’s 300-page IPO filing

Six facts from the filing, in plain English, and what they mean for the tools you run.

Leo Chen · 3 min read · Anthropic · IPO

Anthropic, the company that makes Claude, has filed to go public, and the filing runs more than 300 pages.

Here is what is in it, in plain English.

Going public means the company sells its shares to anyone who wants to buy one. Today those shares belong to the founders, the employees, and large investors. These six facts matter most.

Six facts from the filing

1. A five-seat trust elects most of the board. The seven co-founders also hold one share that carries 50.1% of the vote. People who buy shares in the IPO will not choose the board.

2. Revenue in the first half of 2026 was $16.3 billion. That is about 14 times the $1.2 billion from the first half of 2025.

3. The net loss was $80.7 billion, but $72.4 billion of it is an accounting charge. Investors lent Anthropic money as notes that turn into shares later. As the company’s value rises, the notes are worth more, and accountants record that increase as a loss. No cash left the company.

4. The real cost is computing. Anthropic spent about $9 billion on chips and data centers in six months, which is 55 cents of every dollar it earned.

5. It has signed about $895 billion in contracts, mostly for computing, and about $70 billion of that comes due in the next 12 months.

6. The filing has more than 90 pages of risk factors. They say the technology could pose “catastrophic or existential risks to humanity,” and that insurers may not be able to cover a major AI incident.

As of November 2, the filing has no price range or date. Part of the shares are planned for regular investors through brokers such as Robinhood and Fidelity. That is a fact from the filing, not investment advice.

What it means for you
