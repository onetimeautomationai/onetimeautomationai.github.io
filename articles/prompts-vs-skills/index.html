I Got Sick for Two Days and My Business Did Not Notice

I used to keep a folder of prompts. It was a Rolodex, and I was the index. Which meant nothing in it ran unless I was upright and remembering.

Leo Chen · 5 min read · AI Skills 101

Not the kind of sick where you still answer a few emails from bed. Flat on my back, phone face down, two full working days gone.

I used to keep a folder of prompts for exactly this reason. Good ones. Tested ones. The ones that actually worked, saved and labelled and ready.

It was a Rolodex. I kept it organized, and I was the index.

Which meant nothing in it ran unless I was upright and remembering. A folder of excellent prompts on a day you cannot sit up is worth the same as an empty folder.

What happened during those two days

My inbox still got sorted every morning, and the only messages left sitting there were the ones that actually needed a human.

New leads from my listings still landed in the CRM and still got their first follow up.

Summaries from every call on my calendar were waiting for me, so catching up took minutes instead of all morning.

The client status reports still went out without me.

I opened my laptop on day three braced for the pile. There was no pile.

None of that was a prompt I remembered to run

Every one of them was a skill. Written once, installed, and run by an agent. Many of them on a schedule, which is the part that matters, because a schedule does not care whether I am sick.

A prompt is something you copy and paste. A skill is something you write once and AI runs it forever.

I have 101 of those skill files today and I add more every week.

The actual difference

People hear "skill" and assume it means a better prompt. It does not. The prompt is the smallest part of it.

  • A prompt lives in your memory. A skill lives in a file your agent reads on its own.
  • A prompt starts when you remember it. A skill starts on a trigger: a time, an event, a message arriving.
  • A prompt gives you an answer. A skill finishes a job, including the boring last mile of filing it where it belongs.
  • A prompt gets better when you rewrite it. A skill gets better once, for good, for every run after that.

That last one is the compounding part. Improve a prompt and you have improved today. Improve a skill and you have improved every Tuesday from now on.

The one thing people get wrong. They try to write the perfect skill first. Write the ugly one. Run it. Fix the thing that actually broke. A skill you fixed twice beats a skill you planned for a month.

Start with the thing you keep retyping

Not the hardest thing. Not the most valuable thing. The thing you have typed into a chat window more than three times.

That repetition is the signal. You have already tested it, already know what good output looks like, already know where it goes wrong. All that is missing is writing it down somewhere your agent can find it without you.
