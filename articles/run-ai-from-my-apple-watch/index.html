Run AI from my Apple Watch

One button on my wrist, one way ticket to freedom.

Leo Chen · 3 min read · Build in public

I want to show you what I call a "One way ticket to Freedom" using AI.

Maybe you can relate, because this happens to me all the time.

  • In the car you remember three follow-ups to send. You get home and send none.
  • A great post idea hits while you're at the gym. You tell yourself you'll remember it, and you don't.
  • Your to-do list grows every day, but AI only works while you sit at your desk.

Because AI needs you at a screen, your work waits for you to get back. The ideas you have get lost. You are out living your day, and your head is still at your desk.

For years, I couldn't remember a day when that didn't happen. I even wore holes in my pockets from pulling out my phone all day:

unlock,
tap the notes app,
type into it,
then back in my pocket,

only to pull it out again 6 seconds later.

The good news is that you don't need to keep carrying ideas back to your desk. You just need a better way to hand off the work.

What I say, AI does

So I came up with what I call "What I say, AI does."

What does this mean? I want a one way ticket. No matter where I am or what I'm doing, I can raise my wrist, push one button and talk. Then I know AI takes the notes, writes the emails, fixes the problems, and keeps every idea.

So I set up a Watch-to-AI System that takes action on my instructions, all from my Apple Watch.

Here is how it works. I tap on my watch, say what I want done, and keep going. The memo syncs to Claude. Claude files each memo, writes down the actions, and does the work.

That is how a 47-second voice memo on my hike became an email. It was the one I sent you on how I run 10-20 AI agents at the same time. I gave that instruction from my Apple Watch without typing a word.

In another real memo, I told it my idea for a podcast episode. Then I said "go ahead and write out a very rough draft for it based on the context I gave you. Get that done and send me a iMessage when complete." It added the idea to my content board, wrote the draft, and checked it against my writing rules. None of it needed me at a screen. It was all done and ready for me when I got back to my desk.

What happens after the memo

What happens next is that an iMessage tells you the job finished. Claude wrote the draft, filed the idea, and put the next steps on your list. You sit down at your desk to finished work you started from your watch. Your walks go back to being walks, and you keep your best ideas.

What we don't want while we're living our lives is to be interrupted. That's why the one way ticket is such FREEDOM.
