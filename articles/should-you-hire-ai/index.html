Should You Hire AI?

Before you hire a person, turn “I need help” into a job you can measure.

Leo Chen · 2 min read · Prompt of the Week 08

“I need help” is not a job.

It is a feeling, and hiring a feeling is not a success formula.

Because if you did hire a feeling, you may end up with a person you like, but you cannot describe their work or measure their results.

In business, the lack of performance and the cost compound, and they weigh against your business.

Twenty minutes before the hire

In the AI age, the fix takes about twenty minutes, and it can happen before the hire. Your AI agent reads your calendar and your task list, and then it turns “I need help” into a one-page scorecard. The scorecard includes the mission, three to five measurable outcomes, the first 90 days, and how you will know by day 45 that the hire is not working.

You may even be surprised by how much it helps you find not only clarity, but also tasks that can be automated. Most likely, the job you just described and the job your week shows are not the same job.

The same prompt then writes your day 45 check-in conversation, word for word, in three versions: when it is going well, when it is unclear, and when it is not working.

Why day 45 matters more than day 90

Day 45 matters more than day 90. By day 90, your ego is in it, and most people explain the results away to protect their feelings.
