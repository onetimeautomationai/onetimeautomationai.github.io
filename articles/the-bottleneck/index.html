A Job With Employees Attached

For a long time I told myself my business was busy. Busy was a kinder word than the true one.

Leo Chen · 5 min read · Steps to fix the bottleneck

The true word was that everything had to pass through me. Every yes, every draft, every decision worth more than fifty dollars. Nothing moved unless I touched it.

I called that being involved. Being the one who cares. High standards.

It was a bottleneck wearing a nice outfit.

Here is how I found out

I went away for a few days and did not check in.

When I came back, almost nothing had moved. Not because the people around me are not good. Because I had built a company where the only path forward ran through one person, and that person was on a plane.

Everybody else had been sitting there, ready, waiting on me.

I had not built a business. I had built a job with employees attached.

I assumed that was just how it would always be. It is what most owners assume, and it is why most of them are still doing it ten years later.

I did not get better at delegating

I have tried that. I am bad at it. Hand work to a person and now you are checking on the person, which is the pitfall most businesses never get past. The work left your desk and the worry did not.

What changed was not my discipline. It was the question I asked about everything on my plate.

Not "who could do this," which always ends with me deciding nobody could.

What would have to be true for this to happen without me?

The answer was usually small. Write the workflow down once. Decide what good looks like once. Let something else carry it from there, and improve it when it breaks.

The first one was my inbox and calendar. Then the report I rebuilt every Monday. Then the follow-ups I kept promising and dropping.

Why that question works and the other one does not

"Who could do this" is a question about people, and it invites you to audit the people you have until you find a reason none of them qualify. You always find one. Then you keep the task.

"What would have to be true" is a question about the task. It has an answer, and the answer is a list. Usually three or four things: somewhere the information lives, a rule for what good looks like, a trigger, and one case where a human has to look at it.

Once that list is written down, the handoff is a mechanical step. Before it is written down, no handoff is possible to anyone, human or otherwise, which is why delegation kept failing for me. I was handing over a feeling about how the job should go.

The uncomfortable part

Being the bottleneck feels good. It feels like being important. Every ping is proof that you matter.

Giving that up was harder than any of the technical work. The technical work was never the hard part.

I am still the bottleneck on a few things. The ones that need my judgment, my relationships, my name. Those should be me.

The rest was never supposed to be.
